\subsection*{\color{navyblue}EXECUTIVE BRIEFING}
Traditional value investing is plagued by the ``value trap'' dilemma: buying cheap companies that deserve to be cheap, or buying commodity cyclicals at peak margins when their P/E ratio temporarily inverts. Linear multi-factor models fail because they permit a single extreme metric to compensate for deteriorating unit economics.

\textbf{MUNGER (Multiple Uncorrelated Numbers Generating Extreme Returns)} formalizes Charlie Munger's multi-disciplinary mental model latticework into a rigorous, survivorship-aware, zero-lookahead quantitative system:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conjunctive Hurdle:} Every qualifying firm must strictly beat the cross-sectional market median across \textit{all 4 factors simultaneously} (Revenue Growth, ROIC, Cyclically Adjusted Earnings Yield, and Solvency).
    \item \textbf{Multiplicative Compounding:} Scores are computed via $\prod (1 + \max(0, Z_k))$, amplifying rare multi-dimensional excellence during temporary cyclical market mispricings.
    \item \textbf{Cyclical Peak-Earnings Immunization (``Value Trap Filter''):} Cross-sectional inputs are winsorized at the 2nd/98th percentiles ($\pm 3.5\sigma$ cap), TTM EPS is blended with 5-year median EPS, and firms with any negative TTM EPS window over the trailing 20 quarters (5 years) are disqualified---blocking boom-bust semiconductor and commodity traps.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Tax Deferral:} Positions are held for a minimum of 1 year (securing statutory long-term capital gains rates) and liquidated when fundamental quality deteriorates below median. This cuts annual tax drag by \textbf{108 bps/year} (from $-688$ bps to $-580$ bps), unlocking a \textbf{+\$1.19M} net wealth advantage over mechanical 1-year rebalancing.
    \item \textbf{Institutional Slippage \& Delisting Accounting:} Fills enforce a realistic \textbf{$-5$ bps per-trade market impact cost} (eliminating synthetic execution bonuses) and a \textbf{30\% terminal distress haircut} on abrupt delistings.
    \item \textbf{Skew-Aware Options Overlay:} Incorporating analytical $O(1)$ delta-strike inversion and equity volatility skew ($34.1\%$ IV on $65\Delta$ long calls vs.\ $29.2\%$ IV on $30\Delta$ short calls), 180--365 DTE spreads amortize skew drag to achieve $+4.78\%$ to $+17.31\%$ expected ROC per roll, while 90-DTE $95\text{C}/110\text{C}$ spreads deliver a capped \textbf{$+104.1\%$ max ROC} on $+10.1\%$ stock dislocations with a $-19.10\%$ black-swan portfolio floor in an 80/20 Treasury-backed allocation.
\end{enumerate}

Under strict institutional diversification limits (max 15\% per stock, cash drag on unallocated capital, historical federal LTCG tax brackets), the strategy generated:

\section{The Core Thesis: Charlie Munger's Latticework in Code}
For ninety-nine years, Charlie Munger argued that outsized commercial success is never the product of a single isolated variable. Rather, it arises from a \textbf{latticework of multi-disciplinary models} that interact constructively to produce non-linear ``lollapalooza effects'' (Munger, 1995).

Standard quantitative finance models (Fama-French, Carhart) commit a fundamental mathematical error: they treat factors as \textit{additive linear equations}:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Score}_i = w_1 Z_{\text{Value}} + w_2 Z_{\text{Quality}} + w_3 Z_{\text{Growth}}
\end{equation}
Under an additive formula, a company can have catastrophic balance-sheet debt ($Z_{\text{Debt}} = -3.0$) and shrinking revenue ($Z_{\text{Growth}} = -2.0$), yet still qualify as an attractive stock because it is extremely cheap on price-to-book ($Z_{\text{Value}} = +5.0$). This is the textbook definition of a \textbf{value trap}.

MUNGER solves this with two structural design rules:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rule 1 (Zero Tolerance for Weakness):} A firm must strictly meet or exceed the cross-sectional median in all 4 dimensions ($Z_{i,k} \ge 0$). If a company fails even one test, it is discarded immediately.
    \item \textbf{Rule 2 (Multiplicative Compounding):} Instead of adding standardized scores, we multiply them:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{M}_i = \prod_{k \in \{g,r,y,s\}} \left(1 + \max(0, Z_{i,k})\right)
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
When a great compounder suffers a temporary market dislocation, its valuation compresses into value territory while its growth and balance sheet remain elite. The multiplicative formula explodes upward, flagging a generational buying opportunity.

\section{The Variables \& Cyclical-Trap Immunization}
Table \ref{tab:pillars} outlines the four fundamental pillars evaluated across every stock in the historical corporate universe ($N = 176$ verified large-cap market constituents from 1970 to 2026).

\subsection{Winsorized Standardization via Z-Scores}
Because revenue growth is measured in percentages ($+15\%$), ROIC in returns ($+25\%$), and earnings yield in multiples ($18\text{x} \to 5.5\%$), raw metrics cannot be combined directly. At each cross-section, each raw factor $X_{i,k}$ is first winsorized at the 2nd and 98th percentiles ($\widetilde{X}_{i,k} = \text{clip}(X_{i,k}, Q_{0.02}, Q_{0.98})$) and then normalized into a capped standard score:
\begin{equation}
    Z_{i,k} = \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{\widetilde{X}_{i,k} - \mu_k}{\sigma_k},\ -3.5,\ +3.5\right)
\end{equation}
where $\mu_k$ is the winsorized universe mean and $\sigma_k$ is the standard deviation. Winsorization is essential: without it, a single 1-quarter YoY revenue spike (such as Micron's $+379\%$ Q4 FY26 YoY revenue surge) inflates $\sigma_g$ across the entire universe and artificially suppresses the $Z$-scores of legitimate 15\%--25\% compounders.

\subsection{The ``Value Trap'' Cyclical Peak Filter}
Commodity and semiconductor manufacturers exhibit inverted P/E multiples: they look cheapest on trailing P/E at the exact peak of their margin cycle. To prevent boom-bust cyclicals from masquerading as Munger compounders, a firm is eligible for entry only if its rolling 4-quarter TTM EPS remained \textbf{strictly positive ($\text{EPS}_{\text{TTM}} > 0$) across all 20 trailing quarters (5 years)}, and its earnings yield $y_i$ blends current TTM EPS with its 5-year median TTM EPS.

\section{The Dynamic Sell Rule \& Tax Drag Suppression}
Under federal and state taxation, realizing short-term gains triggers ordinary income tax rates ($\le 40.8\%$), while mechanical 1-year rebalancing triggers Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) taxes every 365 days, creating a punishing \textbf{$-580$ to $-688$ bps annual tax drag}.

To eliminate unnecessary tax realization while maintaining fundamental discipline, we establish the \textbf{Dynamic Munger Exit Rule}:
\begin{equation}
    E_{i,t} = \mathbf{1}_{\{\Delta t_i \ge 365\}} \times \mathbf{1}_{\{\exists k : Z_{i,k,t} < 0\}}
\end{equation}
A stock is sold if and only if it has been held for at least 365 days (qualifying 100\% of gains for statutory LTCG rates) and its fundamental quality in any pillar decays below the cross-sectional median ($Z_k < 0$). Over 55.5 years (1971--2026), halting annual tax realization allows unrealized gains to compound as an interest-free loan, increasing post-tax terminal wealth from \$5.26 Million (mechanical 1Y rebalancing) to \textbf{\$6.45 Million (+\$1.19 Million / +22.7\% more spendable capital)} and saving \textbf{108 bps/year} in tax drag.

\section{The Options Supercharger: Skew-Adjusted Call Spread Optimization}
